Release overview

Previous releases leaned heavily toward historical accuracy. This release deliberately gives gameplay, versatility and strategic importance more weight: technology paths are less rigid, important religions/Wonders can matter earlier, weak or situational civilization-specific assets are replaced by more broadly useful ones, and terrain still matters without deciding combat by itself. Historical theme remains important, but it is no longer followed at the expense of a better game.

The technology tree is the clearest example: Post-Axial Religion, Later Abrahamism and Ecology are replaced by Drama, Sacred Architecture and Automobiles; many unrelated prerequisite links are removed; religion founding is spread across several earlier branches; and research costs are raised to slow the stronger SAS AI. The same principle strengthens civilizations such as Babylon that could lack an impactful early spike: the Babylonian Garden, for example, moves from the culture-oriented Colosseum class to an early Granary replacement that directly supports growth. Other late, coastal or awkward civilization-specific buildings/units receive similar changes, with their concrete costs and effects listed below.

AI work continued through repeated save/autoplay testing. SASGameRecord makes that development loop much easier: run an autoplay, inspect one compact whole-game record, identify a suspicious outcome or possible enhancement, add only the targeted logging/fix needed, then replay and compare. This helped investigate Settlers, Workers, military production, army movement, overseas attacks, war planning, victory denial, Great Generals and several inherited K-Mod/AdvCiv behaviors.

AdvCiv-SAS was also updated to base AdvCiv 1.14. This release ships a first substantial tranche of fixes from a much larger systematic source-archaeology investigation: ChatGPT-5.6-Sol reviewed historical K-Mod, AdvCiv and AdvCiv-SAS runtime-source history commit by commit, using local commit-diff mirrors, Git logs, map/source references and lineage checks to find surviving inherited bugs and SAS regressions. That defined investigation phase is now complete at 131 confirmed findings (43 inherited/pre-SAS and 88 SAS-specific), while implementation is intentionally continuing after this release rather than delaying the release until every deferred finding is fixed. Many of the already-implemented fixes are included below; several SAS-origin fixes have also flowed back into base AdvCiv 1.14, and a related SAS tiny-island map-script bugfix was also adopted into Taurus. The project author and GPT-5.6-Sol (Codex) handled much of the implementation/review and validation, alongside compile/runtime/autoplay testing.

- AI

One of the largest AI/gameplay changes in this release is a deliberate restoration of stronger middle- and late-game military production. AdvCiv-SAS 5055 could produce very large AI armies, whereas later releases generally became much more economically efficient but could end up severely under-militarized, especially late in the game; the investigation also found an inherited Base AdvCiv/K-Mod asymmetry that throttled military production when an AI was strong without providing a corresponding catch-up when an expanding empire became weak for its size. AdvCiv-SAS now combines under-strength catch-up with an XML-tunable offensive production-capacity floor by era. The current Ancient-to-Future defaults are `0 / 0 / 20 / 30 / 35 / 40 / 40%`: Ancient and Classical are deliberately left entirely to ordinary AI logic because early growth/infrastructure was already doing well, and forcing too much military production early can counterintuitively produce a smaller later army by weakening the economic and production base that must sustain it. The floor measures the share of base production currently assigned to `UNITAI_ATTACK` / `UNITAI_ATTACK_CITY` units rather than enforcing a unit quota or cap, and all seven era values can be tuned independently or disabled. See KI#197-KI#197.10.

- Victories (AI)
AI victory denial is much more urgent and reliable. UWAI now recognizes imminent or near-imminent Space victories before the final launch countdown, including sufficiently advanced stage-3 spaceship progress, so a leading rival is less likely to be left in peace until launch simply because the formal countdown has not started yet. See KI#184 and KI#184.2.
The difference is large enough to show clearly in the original test branches: weak Lincoln had previously remained at peace and won Space on turn 292 despite much stronger nearby rivals; after the fixes, Persia, Egypt, France and Babylon declared on turns 267-269 and America was eliminated on turn 272. In another confirming branch, four launched spaceships were stopped by capital captures while the fifth and strongest contender still survived and won, so the system creates serious counterplay rather than guaranteeing that every Space leader is dogpiled or defeated.
Victory-denial war plans are protected from several ordinary-war mechanisms that previously undermined them: normal contact limits no longer block eligible urgent Space wars, war preparations no longer forget why the near-winning rival was chosen, and ordinary peace logic no longer internally accepts a treaty that the victory-denial layer must reject.
After a rival launches a spaceship, AI armies can prioritize capturing its capital before the countdown finishes instead of spending the remaining turns on ordinary cities or routine actions. Before launch, capital capture only moves the capital and does not erase already completed spaceship parts, so this special urgency is correctly focused on the actual post-launch cancellation window. See KI#190.
AI war preparations and target selection are less likely to drift away from a dangerous winner. Several UWAI target-choice and preparation fixes make the AI more willing to attack a viable near-winning rival, less likely to gamble away its own mobilization, and less likely to substitute a clearly worse target after rejecting the best one.

- City Production (AI)
AI military production now has a true under-strength catch-up side instead of only a strong-AI throttle. The AI compares power per city with the city-weighted average of relevant rivals rather than raw empire power, so an overexpanded/under-defended empire can react without a small empire trying to match a much larger rival unit-for-unit. The multiplicative catch-up is capped at +50%; while current unit spending still fits the normal `max + 15` affordability allowance, half of the same capped deficit can also be added as absolute BuildUnitProb points so peaceful personalities with a low base value still respond. In save file 459, Mali was about 34% behind relevant rivals in power per city: its base/XP BuildUnitProb of 29 rose to 39 through the multiplier and then to 56 through the affordable +17 response. See KI#197 and KI#197.8.
Healthy empires now also have an era-progressive minimum share of base-production capacity devoted to offensive `UNITAI_ATTACK` / `UNITAI_ATTACK_CITY` production: Ancient 0%, Classical 0%, Medieval 20%, Renaissance 30%, Industrial 35%, Modern 40%, Future 40%. This is a production-capacity floor, not a unit-count target or an instruction to fill the military-upkeep budget; Worker/Settler/explorer/panic-defense, financial trouble, culture-victory, wait-for-better-units and other earlier priorities still win. The progression deliberately leaves Ancient/Classical development unrestricted and becomes stronger only when empires can otherwise drift into very weak middle/late armies. The later 40% level was chosen after 0/40/100 autoplay comparisons: 40% produced a substantially stronger military than 0% while preserving considerably better infrastructure/economic scaling than 100%. See KI#197.9-KI#197.10.
Several Base AdvCiv/K-Mod building short-circuits now respect that military pressure instead of unexpectedly choosing another building immediately before generic unit production. In one pre-fix test, the early short-circuit chose a building in 10 of 70 evaluations where BuildUnitProb had already reached 100; 6 were completely fresh 0-production buildings. In the confirming rerun, 209 of 211 comparable evaluations rejected the building, and the only 2 successful choices were already 50% and 35% complete, preserving the intended preference to finish worthwhile existing infrastructure rather than starting new infrastructure under maximum military pressure. See KI#197.2 and KI#197.7.
Late floating-defender and generic peaceful city-hunting-stack production now respect military-spending limits more consistently, reducing the risk that stronger military-production logic merely creates another overproduction path. In validation of the city-hunting-stack fix, 391 peaceful over-budget evaluations were blocked while 188 over-budget land-war evaluations remained eligible; none of the actual peaceful stack starts exceeded `max + 15`. The SAS defender-to-offense optimization also no longer rewrites an explicitly required city defender or Settler escort into an offensive UnitAI. See KI#197.3-KI#197.5.
The No Production / process fallback no longer replaces valid Processes with forced military units. In the full-run comparison, fallback military pushes dropped from 410 to 83; all 356 former Process-headed fallback cases disappeared while 86 genuine empty-queue fallback cases remained. SASGameRecord unit-composition counting around this path was corrected at the same time.
Non-combat food-production units are trained by cities that can produce them fast enough without needlessly stalling small growing cities. A nearby mature or stagnant city can take the job instead of leaving a population-1/2 city stagnant for many turns; one logged example was 45 turns for a Worker in population-1 Huamanga versus 3 turns in population-11 Cuzco. Across the confirming run, 87 slow Worker choices were redirected, with the median source at population 2 / 9 turns and the preferred producer at population 8 / 3 turns. A fast-enough local choice is still kept so several cities can replace losses in parallel, and a fallback still exists if every city is slow. See KI#198.2.
Isolated cities also stop producing land civilian units that have no local role: an island Worker is not forced where no plot can be improved, land Explorers are rejected when no exploration remains, and isolated-colony Spies are rejected when there is neither a local rival target nor adjacent transport. This closes a central production-safety gap rather than relying on every individual production branch to remember the exception. See KI#192.
World-Wonder production received two important AI fixes: an inherited K-Mod refactor had inverted the World-Wonder production-city filter and rejected the AI's best production cities, while an AdvCiv-SAS regression had lost game-speed scaling from the cheap-Wonder catch-up thresholds and could almost disable the feature on Slow/Very Slow speeds. Both are fixed. See KI#205.

- Barbarians (AI)
Barbarian city placement was substantially corrected. Multiple inherited Base AdvCiv/K-Mod scoring issues could produce poor sites that normal civilizations would later capture and be forced to live with or raze. Barbarian site evaluation can now use the full workable BFC, considers settlement-specific bonus health/happiness, and no longer lets coast preference, global normal-civilization distance shaping or later area-priority adjustment turn a rejected weak site back into an accepted one. See KI#177 and related follow-ups.
Barbarian and normal-AI city-site bonus scoring now share the improved long-term settlement-oriented valuation instead of broad trade-value logic, so a bonus is judged by its likely health, happiness and practical building value to a city rather than by a generic empire trade valuation.
Barbarian Work Boat handling was fixed so outer workable seafood is considered while docked/queued boats are counted correctly, avoiding both ignored seafood and unnecessary boat flooding. An inherited Better BTS AI/K-Mod area-counting bug that could count the same accessible water area twice was also fixed. See KI#195 and KI#216.
Normal AI armies preparing a real war no longer get diverted into low-priority Barbarian expeditions, and the AI is less willing to perform charity Barbarian cleanup that mostly benefits a rival. See KI#188.4 and KI#188.5.

- Diplomacy (AI)
War and peace decisions were made substantially less volatile. Repeated independent/random reviews could cancel or redirect most war preparations before a planned war ever began, and compounding random peace decisions combined with noisy utility estimates could end profitable wars too readily. The revised logic keeps the AI's chosen war plan more stable while still allowing genuinely bad wars to end. See KI#189 and KI#189.2.
Multi-war peace logic is more selective. An earlier SAS emergency rule could treat any three simultaneous wars as a reason to seek peace with everyone, even when some wars were highly profitable; it now distinguishes the dangerous coalition/problematic fronts rather than abandoning every advantageous war. See KI#65 follow-up.
UWAI target selection no longer rolls independently over candidates in a way that can reject the best target and then accept a much worse rival. Once a preparation is chosen, the AI now selects the rival it actually rates best rather than gambling among weaker alternatives. See KI#191.
The SAS faraway-war guard was narrowed and retuned: it can still discourage clearly remote pre-war targets, but no longer forces advantageous ongoing wars to end, and later tuning made the pre-war distance restriction less invasive when the AI has an overwhelming military opportunity.
AdvCiv's relative-strength technology-trade discount rubber-banding is disabled by default in SAS. A weaker AI recipient no longer receives a guaranteed valuation discount merely because it is weaker, making technology bargaining less mechanically self-correcting and more dependent on the actual deal. The behavior is XML-tunable. See KI#201.
AI strategic-resource trade restrictions were corrected after archaeology found two SAS regressions: the dominated-resource denial applies only to AI recipients, Copper can still be valuable without Iron, and corporation-useful resources are not incorrectly rejected. Human buyers remain unrestricted by these AI-only rules. See KI#239 and KI#240.

- Civilian Units (AI): Settlers (AI)
City-site bonus valuation now uses explicit settlement-oriented long-term value rather than broad `AI_bonusVal` trade logic. Health, happiness and ordinary building effects can be considered through a conservative era gate, while late/expensive or unlikely overlapping buildings are not treated as if they were guaranteed. See KI#178.
Soon-connectable resources are no longer excessively devalued in city-site scoring. Base AdvCiv used blunt roughly 70% / 33% value tiers depending on technology availability; SAS now uses a tunable gradual 15% penalty per era until the bonus can be connected, with no penalty when it is connectable in the current era. This lets the AI recognize a strong bonus that will realistically become useful soon without pretending a several-era-away resource is immediately available. See KI#187.
Reliable duplicate resources already owned or covered by another city's future BFC are no longer over-counted, while genuinely contested or strategically useful duplicates can retain value.
Settler safety/parking was reworked. An exposed Settler no longer founds merely because it is standing on a legal tile, and an unescorted Settler waiting in a safe own city can borrow one healthy local defender when the city can spare it, instead of parking for very long periods despite nearby protection. See KI#179.
A Settler standing on a merely valid current tile now checks whether a clearly better reachable site exists instead of immediately founding a weak filler city. The follow-up path scoring also no longer pulls it back toward a very close but substantially worse site. See KI#180.
Post-capital Settlers can investigate promising nearby fogged alternatives instead of blindly following a stale cached target after geography changes, such as a newly spawned Barbarian city occupying the original expansion space. See KI#185.
The AI is less willing to train an early/midgame Settler when only poor snow/filler-style sites remain, avoiding production spent on expansion that is unlikely to pay back. See KI#181.
Worthwhile island expansion is no longer blocked by the new Settler safety rules, and no-site Settlers/escorts are no longer kept forever by scrap protection. An inherited no-site cleanup bug that could unload unrelated cargo from a transport was also fixed. See KI#194 and KI#215.

- Civilian Units (AI): Workers (AI)
Primary landmasses now maintain a much stronger but deliberately bounded Worker floor. The final XML-tunable policy is effectively 1 Worker at 1 city, 3 Workers at 2 cities, 5 Workers at 3 cities, then +2 Workers for each additional city; demand, production ceilings and Worker-scrapping safeguards use the same target. This replaced the roughly one-Worker-per-city floor that became clearly insufficient. In save file 459, Russia still had only 3 Workers for 6 cities through turn 220, with 39/62 worked plots unimproved at turn 150 and 58/91 at turn 200. The stronger-policy branch had 9 Workers for 4 cities by turn 100 and 11 for 5 cities by turn 150; only 7/54 worked plots were then unimproved and Moscow was 16/16 improved. Further tests showed that an even larger reserve improved land slightly sooner but hurt expansion/population/production, so the final signed reserve is `-1/-1/-1` rather than simply maximizing Worker count. See KI#198.
Worker danger handling is less noisy but more immediately sane: broad inside-border threat scanning no longer causes excessive retreating, while actual danger on the Worker's current plot still matters even inside owned culture. In the save-file-450 comparison this reduced comparable Worker deaths by turn 200 from 40 to 17 while still preventing a Worker from continuing a build on a currently dangerous tile. Worker deaths and retreat context are also easier to diagnose through dedicated logging.
Island/secondary-landmass Worker logistics were reworked. The AI no longer delivers Workers to landmasses that already have enough, requests only the exact shortage, prevents the same unnecessary Worker from repeatedly reboarding an island-bound Settler transport, and can bring Workers home after all useful jobs on a secondary landmass are completed instead of retaining at least one forever. During validation Persia successfully recovered all 5 Workers from completed islands on the same turn, while later demand can still return if new work or Worker losses make the area short again. See KI#192 and follow-ups.
New long-route irrigation planning fixes a post-6020 weakness in the existing Worker system: Workers can search from the actual dry BFC target to irrigation, build the source-side Farm first and recalculate after each link instead of selecting one nearby connector without preserving a complete route. In the Mayapan test a Maize Farm that had remained dry from turn 121 through turn 312 before the fix completed its five-link irrigation route by turn 162. The route search was later corrected so bounded route length remains part of the search state and a valid shorter route cannot be pruned merely because a cheaper but too-long route reached the same plot first. See KI#196 and KI#214.
The AdvCiv 1.14 merge also incorporates the upstream refinement to SAS's stale queued-Worker-mission fix: failed move missions do not execute an improvement on the wrong plot, while route-to missions keep their legitimate along-path route behavior.

- Other Civilian Units (AI)
Great General behavior received another full reliability pass. Great Generals prefer safe birth cities when a safe option exists, the inherited Military Academy city filter no longer rejects safe cities by mistake, travel/path safety no longer confuses ownership-safe routing with tactical safety, and threatened defenseless units no longer select their current unsafe city as a zero-turn retreat and simply skip. See KI#204.
The existing Military Instructor-first policy is now era-tunable and consistent with immediate Military Academy behavior, while rare fallback attachment to low-value ordinary units is heavily gated. A 457-turn post-fix autoplay recorded 91 Great General births: 90 became Military Instructors and 1 died. All 6 Generals whose first AI decision began with enemies inside radius 2 successfully escaped and joined; the sole death was born in Russia's last city on the same turn it was captured and had no AI movement turn before dying. This makes the validation outcome much clearer than merely saying the preference is stronger.
Great General death/history presentation was improved so personal unit names remain visible, player-caused deaths can be persisted into replay/timeline history, and SASGameRecord/BBAI carries birth IDs, coordinates and focused safety/rejection context.

- Military (AI)
AI army organization against Barbarian cities no longer has the inherited threshold inversion that could make an army become "not ready" when adding another attacker. Very large shares of an empire's military could previously wait indefinitely for a weak Barbarian target because a group that was large enough became too large for the old readiness condition. Such forces can now be divided into self-sufficient expeditions instead of endlessly attracting more units. See KI#188.
Capable early attack units can assign an expedition leader and group into a city-capture force when no existing `UNITAI_ATTACK_CITY` unit is present, instead of K-Mod's small-group behavior repeatedly breaking up every possible early Barbarian expedition.
A second inherited targetless-army parking problem was fixed across several individually legal groups. One reproduced case concentrated 71 units - 68% of that AI's entire military - in low-value Mainz while much stronger Aachen and Nuremberg held only 4 units each. Group-level accounting now prevents many separate "small enough" groups from collectively creating a giant useless peacetime stack, while genuine war plans can still assemble large armies. See KI#188.3.2.
Large city-assault armies with no reachable target or war plan no longer wait for reinforcements indefinitely. Units can be released for defense, other expeditions or later wars instead of remaining permanently parked.
Upgrade-city routing now considers whether the AI can actually afford the upgrades. Units no longer gather into one city in huge numbers merely because an upgrade is theoretically available but will not be purchased.
Offensive armies can interrupt or split an expedition to reinforce a nearby endangered own city, and emergency reinforcements now follow through on the rescue mission rather than immediately reverting to unrelated offensive behavior. See KI#188.2.
The AI can prefer a closer weak/disliked land target when UWAI would otherwise prepare against a clearly farther rival for only a small utility difference. The rule remains deliberately narrow and does not rewrite equal-distance, naval or clearly superior target decisions. The separate distance sanity guard was also widened from about 3/12 land/sea path turns to 15/20 so reachable wars are not hard-rejected before normal UWAI has a chance to evaluate them. See KI#61, KI#182 and KI#183.
AdvCiv-SAS's ready-attack upgrade counting no longer evaluates every stack member using only the stack head's UnitAI role. See KI#217.
Dedicated land anti-air units are kept in their intended anti-air role rather than leaking into generic attack/reserve jobs. The problem was large enough to distort whole armies: one turn-350 Saladin force contained 41 Mobile SAMs among only 108 combat units. SAM Infantry/Mobile SAM now stay in their counter/city-counter/special defensive roles instead of being casually reassigned. See KI#199.
Risky stack attacks can deliberately spend SAS-defined obsolete/expendable units before current valuable units when those sacrificial attacks are useful, while retaining tactical-role/value/odds safeguards. In one validation set this overrode inherited sacrifice selection 29 times among 1,426 non-Barbarian attack selections; expendable units could soften defenders so current units then attacked at safer odds rather than being spent first. Very safe attacks remain unchanged. See KI#200.
An inherited K-Mod/Base AdvCiv combat-strength bug was fixed: terrain attack modifiers were reversed in AI attacker-strength estimates when the defender was unknown. This could distort high-level AI expectations even though actual combat resolution used the correct modifiers. See KI#211.

- Military - Naval (AI)
Loaded assault fleets no longer need to reach a generic army-size threshold larger than their own cargo capacity before checking whether their actual loaded force can capture a reachable overseas city. A small but sufficient loaded fleet can therefore attack an achievable island target instead of waiting in port forever. See KI#193.
Coastal cities can now produce only the missing transport capacity required for a nearby worthwhile overseas target when attackers are already available. Existing/queued transports count toward the requirement and ordinary financial, defensive and fleet-limit safeguards remain, so this is an opportunistic logistics fix rather than a general transport spam rule. See KI#193.2.
The naval-trade sea-explorer fallback now counts docked and queued ships and stops once the relevant water area is fully revealed. Before the fix, 25 of 32 actual fallback choices in one run occurred with zero unrevealed water and 22 of 32 occurred despite at least three actual sea-domain ships when docked/queued units were counted; one example built a 200-hammer Attack Submarine into a fully revealed ocean. In the confirming rerun only 8 actual choices remained, and every one had unrevealed water and fewer than three corrected sea units, preserving the fallback where exploration was genuinely still useful. See KI#197.6.

- UI (Sevopedia)
Worker-type units now show their base XML work rate directly as `Work Rate: X%` in shared unit help, including Sevopedia Unit special abilities and normal unit hover help. This makes the Maya Quick Worker, Indian Fast Worker, Netherlands Swift Worker and other variants immediately understandable while separate technology/civic/building build-speed modifiers remain separate.
Executive units now show their required corporation in the Sevopedia Unit Requires panel, with the correct corporation hover and Sevopedia navigation. See KI#236.
Sevopedia Leader item-width handling now honors its documented nonpositive cutoff correctly instead of misapplying the threshold. See KI#230.
Event Trigger era grouping was corrected for indirect unit/building/religion/corporation requirements, so entries such as Man Named Jed, Blessed Sea and Experienced Captain appear under the earliest era in which they can actually become eligible. See KI#225.
Vote effects no longer duplicate trade-route bonuses as requirements, and Event pillage bounds are labeled as improvement counts rather than gold. See KI#226 and KI#227.

- UI (In-game)
The Military Advisor's leader-button sizing is now fit-aware. On narrow maps/low resolutions with many living players (including SAS48 cases), it starts from the normal 64 px buttons and reduces them only as much as required so the complete leader list fits instead of overflowing vertically.
Foreign Advisor Treaties rows now show every term that their cancellation action will actually affect: mixed resource/treaty or resource/GPT deals display all resource and non-resource terms together, while resource-only deals remain intentionally filtered. Bonus, gold and leader icons plus localized trade-direction text make those mixed deals easier to scan. See KI#241.
Aligned scoreboard scrolling was fixed so scrolling, active-player centering, button state and drawing all use the same post-sort/post-Max-Players order while preserving aligned-vassal grouping. See KI#228.
Domestic Advisor founding-year sorting is chronological again rather than lexicographic, and the Free Colony/Liberate action lost during the SAS advisor-shell refactor has been restored. See KI#223 and KI#233.
World Advisor vassal perspective no longer leaks unrevealed map information: plot revelation, bonus knowledge, improvements and routes use the active human observer's information rather than raw state from the selected vassal. Debug mode remains intentionally unrestricted. See KI#224.
Policy Advisor corporation yield/commerce values are correctly converted from stored hundredths and retain world-size scaling, and vassal perspectives preserve the active player's actual map visibility. See KI#231 and KI#232.
Foreign Advisor Espionage no longer keeps a stale target/city/mission after the selected rival disappears; it validates the current met/alive list and picks a valid target. See KI#234.
Stealth Bomber/air-unit hover help again displays relevant collateral information without reintroducing irrelevant civilian or nuke collateral noise. See KI#235.
Great Person probability text fitting now uses the actual runtime bar width and rejects extra candidates before they overflow. The inherited fixed-width/Maximum-types fitting bugs are fixed. See KI#237 and KI#237.2.
Hall of Fame date sorting again uses date-typed headers while retaining the scalable label font. See KI#229.
Military Summary now respects anarchy's waived unit-support costs and includes free Great-General upgrades in minimum/average upgrade-cost calculations. See KI#219 and KI#220.
Battle-history capture attribution was corrected so city captures require the lethal battle on the captured city's exact plot and civilian captures match the relevant escort-clearing battle by plot/player direction rather than by civilian unit type. See KI#221 and KI#222.

- Map Generation (Common)
Standard shared map generation is now much more configurable through independent XML SAS defines. Players/modmods can select BTS alternatives for individual shared generation steps such as standard terrain/features, fractal defaults, latitude, coastal Peaks, rivers, lakes and bonus placement/distribution without recompiling the DLL. Default AdvCiv-SAS behavior remains the AdvCiv path. See the map-generation define block in `GlobalDefines_advciv_sas.xml`.
AdvCiv's OneArea-resource fallback was fixed. If the capped first pass undershoots food/luxury resource targets, the uncapped second pass can now revisit a landmass already attempted instead of being unable to fill the existing target. XML resource frequencies/targets are unchanged; only previously missed placements can be recovered. See KI#202.

- Handicaps
Animal density was rebalanced across the full Rookie -> Deity+ ladder. `iUnownedTilesPerGameAnimal` changed from `55/52/49/46/43/40/37/34/31/28/25` to `65/62/59/56/53/50/47/44/41/38/35`; higher values mean fewer animals, so the highest handicaps now have substantially fewer animals while the lower difficulties remain more gradual.
The low-handicap AI creation-cost curve was also made evenly progressive: `iAICreatePercent` for Rookie/Settler/Chieftain/Warlord changed from `108/106/104/102` to `112/109/106/103`.

- Technologies
The technology-tree rework is mainly about making strategic branches more distinct rather than forcing technologies into the strictest possible historical order. Three existing technology concepts were replaced rather than added on top: Post-Axial Religion -> Drama, Later Abrahamism -> Sacred Architecture, and Ecology -> Automobiles.
Automobiles is the clearest example of the new approach. Fascism had become an unusually overloaded beeline: one political technology also carried Infantry/Machine Gun and the armored Tank/Counter Tank path. Fascism now keeps its political effects plus Infantry and Machine Gun, while Automobiles costs 2600, requires Fascism + Combustion, unlocks Tanks and Counter Tank, and leads toward Industrialism. This deliberately makes the political and armored choices more distinct instead of putting too much military value on Fascism itself.
Drama replaces Post-Axial Religion with a concrete early cultural node: it costs 260, requires Polytheism and unlocks the culture slider/process, Theatre and Natya Shastra. Sacred Architecture costs 650, requires Drama + Monotheism and leads toward Aesthetics, forming a clearer religion/culture/wonder branch. Ecology was removed as a separate technology and its remaining assets were redistributed mainly among Theory of Evolution, Environmentalism and Composites rather than keeping another late technology merely to preserve the previous tree shape.
Religion founding was intentionally spread across earlier and different strategic branches so more religions have time to affect a game and the same first religion is less likely to dominate every run. Hinduism moves earlier to Calendar; Judaism now rewards the legal Code of Laws path; Christianity becomes the Monotheism race; and Islam moves to Navigation, giving a naval/exploration path a religious payoff. Organized Religion also moves earlier to Polytheism so missionary spread can begin sooner, while Theocracy moves to Monotheism and Monasteries to Calendar.
Several genuinely unrelated cross-branch requirements were removed for the same reason. Civil Service no longer requires Iron Working, so Bureaucracy can be pursued without automatically entering the heavy-melee branch (Macemen still keep their own Iron Working requirement). Engineering no longer requires Iron Working, allowing Crossbows/Trebuchets to form a real alternative to the melee branch. Military Science no longer requires Democracy; Assembly Line no longer requires Economics so Steam Power remains a versatile production path; Advanced Flight no longer requires Fascism so the Gunship counter to Tanks is not itself locked behind a political branch; and Fission no longer requires Artillery, keeping the nuclear path parallel rather than forcing an artillery beeline. Future-era AI/Bioengineering/Fusion/Space Exploration prerequisites were similarly simplified where Depopulation already guarantees the relevant modern branches, and Transhumanism can now be reached through either AI or Bioengineering.
Other timing moves follow the same idea of putting powerful effects on technologies where they create a useful choice. Slavery moves from Agriculture to Masonry, keeping Agriculture less overloaded; Pacifism moves from Meditation to Medicine, strengthening a weaker later technology instead of stacking another powerful effect on the early religious path; Spy and Scotland Yard move together to Monarchy; Granary-class buildings move from Agriculture to Pottery; Plantation building moves to Agriculture; Jungle removal moves to Metal Casting so Masonry does not carry Slavery plus both Forest/Jungle clearing; and fallout cleanup moves to Environmentalism.
Research costs were increased because the stronger SAS AI was progressing through the tree too quickly in autoplay; in one Monarch/Large/Pangaea sample several civilizations had already reached the Industrial era by turn 201. For an exact old/new column comparison, the new shared 260-cost column is written twice below even though it is only one visual timing tier in the current tree:
old: 40/65/100/150/225/300/425/600/800/1050/1300/1650/1900/2100/2250/2400/2500/2650/2800/3000
new: 50/80/120/175/260/260/425/650/850/1100/1400/1700/1900/2100/2300/2500/2600/2700/2800/3000
The duplicated 260 is only for easier column-by-column comparison with 6020. The actual current tree visually merges those former two columns. The first increase was slightly stronger (125/275/450 in the early/mid tiers) and was then eased to the final 120/260/425 values.
Several unit/building unlocks moved with the new structure. Catapults are at Mathematics and Trebuchets/Crossbowman-class units at Engineering; Counter Tank moves with Tanks to Automobiles; Cereal Mills moves from Environmentalism to Theory of Evolution, giving the Biology/scientific path an economic payoff while Corporation remains required. Liberalism is tradeable again after the revised reward/tree structure made its old no-trade protection unnecessary; Naturalism remains protected.
Technology audio/text validation was added alongside the tree rework so every technology keeps a valid distinct spoken quote/script and Civ4-incompatible typography is caught automatically.

- Terrains / Features
All unit terrain/feature combat modifiers were rebalanced and generally tamed so geography influences combat without overpowering unit quality or city warfare. Terrain and Hills now normally use 10%/20% tiers with a hard 20% cap because they can affect city combat; Features use 10%/20%/40% tiers because features normally disappear from city plots. Civ-specific units generally keep one strong terrain/feature specialization or at most two weaker terrain affinities, while generic units use lighter effects; overly broad attack+defense packages were reduced, unintended positive defense was removed from most `bNoDefensiveBonus` mounted/camel/elephant units, and dead combat modifiers were removed from noncombat Workers. The full per-unit values and rationales remain documented in `README_Assets_Rebalancing.md`.

- Bonuses
Livestock resources (Cattle, Sheep and Pig) now have more secondary strategic uses beyond tile yield and base health: Temple of Artemis gives +1 happiness from each Cattle/Sheep/Pig, the Sumerian Ziggurat keeps +1 health from Sheep, the Mongol Ger gains +1 happiness from Cattle, and the Roman Forum gains +1 happiness from Pig.
Camel and Deer yields were rebalanced. Camel changes from a base 3 food plus Pasture +1 food/+2 hammers/+2 commerce to base 2 food plus Pasture +2 food/+1 hammer/+2 commerce; on Desert this gives a final 4 food/+1 hammer/+2 commerce tile instead of overloading the unimproved resource. Deer changes from base +1 food with Camp +3 food/+1 commerce to base +2 food with Camp +2 food/+1 commerce, preserving the same improved +4 food while making unimproved Deer regions less food-poor.

- Buildings
World-Wonder timing was retuned together with the technology tree so Wonders appear early enough for their effects to matter; most associated cost changes are therefore timing adjustments rather than isolated buffs/nerfs.
Several important early Wonders now arrive sooner. The Pyramids move from Masonry to Mining and 150 -> 125 production, giving this very early Wonder more time to matter. Temple of Artemis drops the Construction requirement and becomes a Polytheism-only Wonder, 200 -> 150 production, which gives Polytheism a stronger identity while reducing the old pile-up on Construction. Temple of Kukulkan moves from Engineering to Construction, 275 -> 200 production and obsoletes at Chemistry instead of Gunpowder, giving its effect a substantially longer useful window. Colossus 175 -> 150 follows Metal Casting moving earlier, while Parthenon 225 -> 200 follows Code of Laws moving earlier. Natya Shastra moves to Drama and changes 350 -> 250 production in the final shared timing tier.
The Apostolic Palace is a more strategic change than a normal timing adjustment: Constitution -> Philosophy, 300 -> 275 production, plus one free Priest. This makes religious diplomacy available sooner, gives otherwise weaker Philosophy another major reason to research it, and gives the builder an immediate reward/Great Prophet progress alongside effects that are largely shared with the religion. The United Nations separately drops from 1500 -> 1200 production: it remains much more expensive than ordinary late Wonders, but its diplomatic-victory role is now practical enough to pursue intentionally.
Other final Wonder cost/timing adjustments since 6020 are Mausoleum of Maussollos 275 -> 250 (and Aesthetics -> Currency, so Golden Age extension can affect more of the game); Hanging Gardens 225 -> 250 after its later Mathematics timing; Spiral Minaret 400 -> 350 after Sacred Architecture moves earlier; Globe Theatre 475 -> 400 and Hermitage 500 -> 400 after moving to Aesthetics; Oxford University and Sankore 450 -> 400 after Broader Education moves earlier; Mount Rushmore 625 -> 600 after Fascism moves earlier; Statue of Liberty 500 -> 525 after moving Democracy -> Liberalism; Cristo Redentor 525 -> 550 after moving Liberalism -> Romanticism; and Moai Statues 475 -> 500. These are kept here for completeness even when the important change is the new timing rather than the number itself.
World-Wonder culture is now standardized more consistently by timing: 4 in the first two Ancient columns, 6 through the rest of Ancient/Classical, and 8 through Medieval/Renaissance; later Wonders receive flat culture only when it fits their role. Broadway's culture modifier returns from 25% to 50%. Great Person sources were also diversified: Temple of Artemis Great Prophet -> Great Merchant, Parthenon Great Prophet -> Great Spy, Hanging Gardens Great Artist -> Great Scientist, Statue of Zeus Great Engineer -> Great Artist, Moai Great Prophet -> Great Engineer, Eiffel Tower Great Artist -> Great Scientist and Pentagon Great Spy -> Great Engineer. Temple of Artemis also loses its former +10% military-production class modifier.
Civilization-specific buildings received a large gameplay-focused pass. The net changes below deliberately compare the last stable 6020 state with the current release state; short-lived intermediate designs are ignored. The main goal is for a unique building to shape ordinary games instead of arriving too late, depending heavily on map geography, or forcing a leader down a path that does not fit its kit. Historical theme still guides the identity, but the building class is chosen for gameplay usefulness when a literal analogue would make the civilization weaker or more situational. More detailed rationale remains in `README_Assets_Rebalancing.md`.
Arabia: Madrassa (University) -> Furusiyya Maydan (Stable). The 60-production Stable keeps normal +2 mounted XP, adds two Priest slots and +1 health from Horse and Camel. Saladin's Spiritual/Charismatic, military-leaning profile should not be forced through a University/science route merely to access its civilization-specific effects: the Stable directly supports mounted warfare and the later camel-based Hajjan, while the Priest slots retain the religious identity. Horse/Camel health also complements Charismatic happiness by letting cities grow farther before health becomes limiting and keeps the livestock useful after its military window.
Babylon: Garden (Colosseum) -> Garden (Granary). The 45-production Garden keeps 40% food, normal cereal health, +1 permanent health, +1 additional health from Banana and +1 happiness from Grapes/Sugar. Babylon was relatively weak in broad early-game impact compared with stronger ancient civilizations; putting its UB on very early growth infrastructure gives Hammurabi a meaningful spike and a broadly useful tool for recovering population after Slavery or warfare rather than another building on the culture/entertainment path.
Carthage: Cothon (Port) -> Punic Agora (Grocer). The 110-production Punic Agora is usable inland, keeps normal Grocer health/happiness, raises gold from +15% to +35% and Merchant slots from 2 to 3. This removes the Cothon's strong coast/map dependency while preserving Hannibal's commercial identity; the stronger Gold/Merchant package can fund either research or military expansion without depending on foreign trade.
Germany: Longhouse (Granary) -> Zunfthaus (Forge). The 100-production Zunfthaus gives +35% general production and two Engineer slots. The 6020 Longhouse's early Granary/flat-food identity was both very strong and generic; moving the advantage to a Forge creates a more distinctive craft/manufacturing identity that either German leader can use for infrastructure, units, Wonders, research-supporting buildings or later space goals.
Ireland: Scriptorium (University) -> Muileann Uisce (Aqueduct). The 90-production Aqueduct adds +1 hammer to worked river plots and +1 happiness from Wheat. Ireland's civ-specific unit arrives late, so the UB now supplies a meaningful earlier economic/production identity; it also avoids forcing Grace O'Malley or Michael Collins through the former religious/science route merely to benefit from Ireland's unique infrastructure.
Netherlands: Dike (Levee) -> Beurs (Bank). The 200-production Beurs keeps normal +50% gold, adds +50% trade-route yield and an additional +100% foreign trade-route yield. The Dike arrived late and could be nearly irrelevant on the wrong geography; the Bank works on every map but is late/expensive enough that its compounding trade bonuses remain an investment. Domestic trade gives a reliable floor during war or isolation, while diplomacy/open foreign trade gives the larger payoff; the already-strong Swift Worker supplies the civilization's earlier spike.
Portugal: Feitoria (Custom House) -> Peixaria (Grocer). The 110-production Peixaria keeps normal Grocer effects and adds +1 health from Fish/Molluscs plus +1 happiness from Crab/Whale in every city. It preserves Portugal's maritime/resource identity without requiring the city itself to be coastal, making marine resources relevant throughout an empire and helping Joao sustain growth or warfare instead of relying on another late, situational naval-trade building.
Russia: Gord (Castle) -> Prikaz (Courthouse). The 110-production Prikaz keeps normal -50% maintenance and adds +3 espionage, +25% espionage and two assignable Spy slots. Russia commonly plays as a large empire and its leaders can make meaningful use of espionage; the Courthouse supports expansion directly while the intelligence package creates an alternative strategy rather than another late science multiplier. Spy slots keep Great Spy generation optional instead of automatically polluting every city's Great Person pool.
Scandinavia: Trading Post (Port) -> Chieftain's Hall (Barracks). The 50-production Barracks keeps normal land XP, gives newly trained units free City Raider I and reduces local war weariness by 10%. A map-dependent naval building is replaced by early offensive infrastructure that directly supports Ragnar and the later Viking raider, while the modest war-weariness reduction helps sustain campaigns without becoming a general economic bonus.
USA: Mall (Supermarket) -> Federal Courthouse (Courthouse). The 80-production building reduces maintenance by 60% instead of the normal 50% and adds +25% espionage defense. The Mall arrived too late to shape most games; the Courthouse instead helps the commonly large American empires integrate newly founded or conquered cities, while the security effect adds identity without simply stacking another economic multiplier. The Holy Roman Rathaus remains the stronger pure-maintenance building at -70%.
Native America: Totem (Monument) -> Soldiers' Lodge (Jail). The 110-production Soldiers' Lodge keeps normal -25% war weariness, raises espionage to +50%, gives two Spy slots and +50% espionage defense. The short-lived Monument/Totem identity suited Sitting Bull poorly; the Jail timing and active/counter-espionage package fit his high espionage preference, Philosophical/Protective traits, cautious diplomacy and defensive posture without simply adding another military bonus.
Khmer: Baray (Aqueduct) -> Hall of Dancers (Theatre). The 60-production Theatre keeps the stronger culture-slider happiness step and adds +1 happiness from Elephants. This replaces another fairly generic health building with a more distinctive ritual/cultural tool: Suryavarman's Creative/cultural path can turn culture investment into happiness/growth, while Elephants remain useful after their military window.
India: Mausoleum (Jail) -> Mausoleum (Colosseum). The 90-production Colosseum keeps normal culture/happiness and adds +1 happiness from Gold. A late espionage/security Jail fit both the monumental Mausoleum and India's kit poorly; the earlier Construction-path Colosseum lets Spiritual Gandhi/Asoka turn cultural/religious development into sustained city happiness and growth while remaining useful on the same broad infrastructure/siege path.
Spain: Citadel (Castle) -> Lonja (Market). The 110-production Market adds +1 trade route and +1 happiness from Silver. Siege XP on a defensive Castle was an awkward offensive perk with a short window; the Market supports Spain's imperial/commercial play throughout the game, keeps a domestic trade benefit when warfare disrupts foreign trade and gives Silver another strategic use.
Greece: Odeon (Colosseum) -> Odeon (Theatre). The 60-production Theatre keeps +1 happiness and +1 Great Artist point and uses Dyes for its resource happiness. This fits an Odeon better, fills an otherwise underused building class and sits naturally on Greece's religion/culture path without duplicating the Khmer culture-slider identity.
Japan: Doujou (Jail) -> Doujou (Barracks). The 50-production Barracks keeps normal +3 land XP, adds one Spy slot and gives +15% empire-wide Great General rate per Doujou. Moving from a late Jail to early military infrastructure makes the building matter during Japan's actual military development, while the Spy slot preserves part of the intelligence/isolationist identity and the Great General effect rewards broad martial infrastructure.
Korea: Gyeongdang (Library) remains Gyeongdang (Library), but is rebalanced. It keeps three Scientist slots; its former +10% Great General modifier is replaced by free Combat I for newly trained units. This represents combined civil/martial education more directly and saves one promotion step without letting an early, widely built Library immediately bypass the first Combat tier.
Other civ-specific balance changes were also made without replacing the final building identity. Aztec Sacrificial Altar changes hurry anger -75% -> -50% and replaces its flat +1 happiness with +1 happiness from Maize, shifting power from an automatic early bonus into the Slavery/Granary/Maize regrowth loop. Byzantine Hippodrome loses culture-slider happiness and gains -20% war weariness, making its Stable role support Cataphract warfare directly. Persian Apothecary changes flat health 2 -> 1, Spices health +1 -> +2 and gains +10% research, making medicinal resources/science more distinctive than a simply healthier Grocer. Maya Ball Court moves its resource happiness from Maize to Gemstones, making more of its power conditional and giving Maya a prestige-resource objective. Roman Forum local Great Person modifier +50% -> +25%, adds +1 happiness from Pig and +1 Great Scientist point, reducing an unusually large unconditional early GPP multiplier while keeping gradual Great Person/economic value. Mongol Ger cost 70 -> 60, mounted XP 3 -> 2, gains +1 happiness from Cattle and its narrow military-production modifier rises +10% -> +15%, trading some raw mounted XP for broader pastoral value. Zulu Ikhanda cost 65 -> 50 because raising the cost of its individually modest maintenance/military effects made Shaka noticeably less competitive in the crucial early game. Ottoman Hammam cost 100 -> 90, flat happiness 2 -> 1, and gains +1 happiness/+1 health from Marble, making part of its strong effect conditional while giving Marble lasting value after Wonder construction. English Stock Exchange gains +2 Great Merchant points so the late financial building retains a permanent distinction even when Merchant specialist slots become widely available.
Related ordinary-building balance was adjusted too: Castle cost 110 -> 130 because its defensive/economic package was too cheap to establish before invasions; University and French Salon 200 -> 190 because the streamlined later tree gives less time to repay their cost; Aqueduct 80 -> 90 because broadly useful early health was too inexpensive; Intelligence Agency 180 -> 200 and loses its flat 8 espionage while retaining +50% espionage/two Spy slots; and the narrow military-production modifiers on Ger, Mount Rushmore and Pentagon were normalized upward from +10% to +15% so they remain meaningful beside buildings with stronger general-production effects.

- Units
The civ-specific unit pass follows the same general goal: replace narrow or strongly map-dependent advantages with tools that can matter in more games, while keeping exact combat identities visible.
Portugal: Carrack (Galleon) -> Cacador (Grenadier). The Cacador keeps the Grenadier's 12 strength, 120 production, +25% vs Gun, +10% city attack and light collateral; it adds free Combat III, one first-strike chance, raises withdrawal 20% -> 25%, and adds +10% attack/defense on Grass and Forest. The main purpose is to replace a strongly map-dependent naval UU with an elite land unit useful on every map. Combat III is deliberately the main enduring perk because veteran upgrades are less likely to waste it on a low promotion already owned, and it persists into later upgrades; the smaller first-strike/withdrawal/terrain effects give the Cacador a light-infantry/skirmisher identity without simply inflating raw strength.
Maya: Holkan (Spearman) -> Quick Worker (Worker). The Quick Worker costs the same 60 production as a normal Worker, keeps 2 movement and normal movement rules, but raises work rate 100% -> 125% (+25%). Pacal/Maya repeatedly tended to underperform or disappear early in autoplay, while the Holkan remained a narrow short-window military advantage and the Ball Court is itself specialized. Faster resources, roads, improvements and chops instead strengthen Maya broadly and can support expansion, economy, Wonders or military logistics, while remaining distinct from India's terrain-cost immunity and the Netherlands' extra-movement Swift Worker.
With Portugal's Carrack now replaced, and the Netherlands already using the Swift Worker from the previous stable release, playable civilizations currently have no naval unique units. This avoids a UU becoming nearly irrelevant on land-heavy maps or disproportionately decisive on naval-heavy maps when most civilizations have no comparable naval counterpart.
Worker production cost was restored from the 6020 SAS value of 50 to the Civ4/K-Mod/Base AdvCiv 60-production baseline, including Indian Fast Worker, Netherlands Swift Worker and Maya Quick Worker. The earlier reduction made additional Workers increasingly cheap and less strategically meaningful once cities became productive. The AI noncombat food-production allocator's turn gate moved 5 -> 6 at the same time, preserving approximately the same 10 production-equivalent-per-turn qualification (`50 / 5 = 60 / 6 = 10`) instead of accidentally making secondary Worker pumps harder to use.
Other concrete unit balance changes include:

- Ottoman Janissary (Musketman): free Combat II -> Combat III and gains +10% city attack. Its former strong Grass specialization is replaced by only +10% Plains/Desert attack/defense, moving more of the UU's strength into a broadly useful elite-infantry package.

- Grenadier: withdrawal 15% -> 20%.

- Longbowman: production 45 -> 40, city attack 10% -> 0%, city defense 50% -> 25%; final terrain identity is +20% Hills defense.

- Babylonian Bowman (Longbowman): production 45 -> 40, city defense 25% -> 10%, free City Raider I -> Retreat I; final specialization is Desert +20% attack/+10% defense plus +20% Hills defense.

- Mali Skirmisher (Longbowman): city defense 25% -> 10%; final Plains/Desert affinities are +10% attack/defense and Hills defense +20%.

- Chinese Zhuge Nu (Crossbowman): two guaranteed first strikes -> one guaranteed + one chance first strike; collateral remains 15 with its free collateral promotion.

- Holy Roman Houfnice (Cannon): loses the free City Bombard Damage promotion; the promotion remains selectable with experience, forcing a tradeoff against City Raider/collateral/survival promotions instead of granting both bombardment and assault specialization automatically.

- Machine Gun: production 130 -> 150; its strong specialized defensive package is otherwise retained.

- Modern Tank: production 270 -> 280 and loses its guaranteed first strike.

- Robotic Tank: production 320 -> 340.

- Lion strength 4 -> 3 with +20% Plains/+10% Grass attack; Bear 5 -> 4 with +20% Forest attack; Panther gains +20% Jungle attack; Wolf gains +20% Snow/+10% Tundra attack.

- Warrior/Ancient Maceman animal bonus 50% -> 33%.
Terrain/feature packages were rebalanced separately under the shared rules above rather than listed unit by unit here; exact current values remain in Sevopedia/XML and the Assets Rebalancing guide.
Worker-type units now expose their base XML work rate directly in normal hover help and Sevopedia, making differences such as the Quick Worker's 125% immediately visible.

- Promotions
Legacy BTS promotion text keys were renamed to match the current AdvCiv-SAS promotion names (for example the old Commando text key now matches Logistics). Promotion types/gameplay are unchanged by this text-key cleanup.
Counter Tank now unlocks at Automobiles alongside Tanks rather than Artillery, so the specialized counter cannot appear before its intended armored target and no longer adds another military reward to Fascism.

- Other issues fixed/improved
The final post-6020 Known Issues sweep found two fixed/improved issues not already described elsewhere in this changelog:
Captured-city keep/raze logic no longer uses Base AdvCiv's crude `AI_isAwfulSite` veto, which could destroy valuable developed cities only for the AI to spend another Settler rebuilding on the exact same tile. The replacement asks whether the existing city is likely to benefit the recipient long-term, combining more consistent intrinsic site quality with sustainable same-area distance while leaving population, buildings and broader developed-city value to normal conquest/trade valuation. In the clearest pre-fix case, Ur, Tours and Augsburg were successively founded and razed on the same tile; after the fix, HRE kept Ur and it grew to population 13. See KI#186.
Active World Wonders no longer automatically override the long-term distance sanity check for captured cities. A nearby Wonder city remains protected by its strategic value, but a remote one can now be razed when it is too exposed to hold durably, denying the Wonder instead of exhausting defenders and handing the developed city back to a nearer rival. Testing reproduced the old failure with England committing 15 defenders to remote Elephantine only for Egypt to recapture it six turns later after killing at least seven defenders. See KI#186.2.

- Code/Performance optimizations (no gameplay change unless stated otherwise above)
Added SASGameRecord, a new separate structured `SASGameRecord_*.log` for compact whole-game/autoplay review. Its main purpose is to make AI development more self-feeding: run an autoplay, inspect one compact factual record, notice a suspicious outcome or possible enhancement, add only the targeted logging or code change needed to investigate it, then run the same kind of test again and compare the result. It records economy, expansion, city/battle history, worked plots, unit composition, exploration, map/environment changes, wars and their causes, diplomacy memories, all victory progress, spaceship/project state, production/military flows, civic/religion/war-plan transitions, technology-acquisition provenance, Barbarian activity, run/session context and explicit autoplay end causes.
SASGameRecord can also produce optional level-3 text-map previews with terrain, features, bonuses, directional rivers and quantitative legends. Natural food quality is reflected through terrain-map case, and rendered reference screenshots are included in the light-source archive so LLMs can understand the geographical layout more reliably than from raw character grids alone.
Unattended AI Auto Play was made more reliable for long validation runs, and SASGameRecord now records explicit run outcomes so completed victories, turn-limit endings, autoplay interruption and other termination states are distinguishable during later review. See KI#203.
The existing dedicated Settler BBAI category was expanded with parking reasons, current danger context and city escort pools; new dedicated categories were added for Great Generals, war/UWAI, overseas transports and military production. This keeps each investigation focused without enabling broad multi-megabyte unit/city/team logging.
The light-source archive helper was expanded from a convenient source ZIP into a reproducible review snapshot. `_SNAPSHOT_CONTEXT` records current branch/HEAD/commit count and working-tree state, staged/unstaged diffs, ignored paths, recent Git history and filtered historical commit diffs across K-Mod -> pre-SAS AdvCiv -> SAS branch ancestry, plus path-to-commit indexes. The archive can therefore support offline/ZIP-only source archaeology without shipping the full `.git` repository.
A tracked filtered historical commit-diff mirror and privacy-preserving immutable-SHA cache were added so clone/code-agent workflows can reuse the same archaeology data efficiently. Email-shaped strings in historical patch text are redacted under the same privacy policy as the anonymized Git logs.
The light-source helper now also tracks fetched-but-unmerged upstream AdvCiv release history separately, understands exact MERGE_HEAD during an active merge, can explicitly fetch upstream refs, and avoids mixing future upstream commits into current-HEAD ancestry.
Added a dependency-free `convert_advciv_manual_to_txt.py` helper so the searchable `manual.txt` copy can be regenerated reproducibly from AdvCiv's `manual.odt` content rather than through PDF extraction or an external office suite.
CI/build checks were expanded: repository-local Markdown links/images, Civ4 GameText character compatibility, technology audio resolution/distinctness, XML redundant/default forms, civ-specific mapping and text/Pedia references, plus existing XML/Python/world-size/predump/build safety checks. Python code is still checked with Ruff and real CPython 2.4 compilation.
The release process itself now has a living repository checklist for upstream merge review, DLL validation, screenshot/documentation audit, cumulative changelog construction, tracked history/light-source refresh, final release review and user-controlled tagging/publication.
