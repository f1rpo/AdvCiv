- AI
	Note: many of the changes have been made with the iterative loop of: add more BBAI logging (disabled if the SAS define is disabled for perf opt), test it ingame via autoplay, give the output or visual view result to LLMs like ChatGPT-5.5 or GPT-5.5 on Codex (and have them review each other or compare their solutions), hunt for AI issues (or bugs), blunders, or enhancements (these logs can be very long), then implement a cure or add more logging if more information is needed and test it ingame via autoplay, give the output or visual view result to LLMs, find more issues, etc.

	This helped make the AI in this AdvCiv-SAS 6020 (feddabdf89f022dcf6412aab0cab414a54b4610c) release much stronger than previous AdvCiv-SAS 5860 Update 4 release (82f042a44f4cd4bbc2b687444356d220b83131c1, which is in fact AdvCiv-SAS 5912).

- Victories (AI)
	Note: cultural victory bullets as of now assume 3 legendary cities are required, if this is changed in a mod mod or such the below explanation would need to be adjusted accordingly.

	- Cultural-victory AI is viability and bottleneck-focused: The new `SAS_BBAI_CULTURE_LOG_LEVEL` BBAI logging showed Base AdvCiv could treat an AI raising the culture slider only for happiness or border pressure as evidence that it was pursuing a cultural victory. Even the early preparation stage could then raise culture priorities in every city. As a result, the AI could spend production, specialists, and commerce on cities culturally ranked lower than the cultural-victory target group (i.e., the top 3 cities that must reach Legendary culture), on a target city already much closer to Legendary than another target city, or even on a city that had already become Legendary. This was inefficient and could leave the AI dangerously underinvested in stronger economic, scientific, or military priorities. To address these issues, the AI no longer treats its culture slider alone as a cultural-victory commitment, and early preparation no longer raises culture priority throughout the empire. Before serious cultural victory pursuit begins, at least 2 of the 3 target cities must now each have already accumulated a tunable share of the Legendary requirement (10% by default), and the AI must rank among the configured number of leading cultural contenders (No point pursuing culture if we won't win culturally anyway). Once committed, only the target cities receive victory-specific culture weight, shifted toward the city projected to take longest (no point having a city have 70% legendary culture and another 10% legendary culture only, as several legendary cities are required to win culturally, so have the spread be more even); a city that is already Legendary keeps only independent local-border value. Converting production to culture remains available for first-ring expansion and strongly contested borders, but otherwise only to active target cities.
	- Hopeless late cultural pursuits are abandoned: The new `SAS_BBAI_CULTURE_LOG_LEVEL` BBAI logging also showed a late cultural pursuit in which the least advanced target city was projected hundreds of turns from Legendary when the estimated game had little more than 100 turns remaining (e.g., Future Era at T~400 at Normal game speed), despite likely faster Space or other victory routes. The AI now returns to preparation when its maximum affordable culture-slider projection misses the estimated deadline beyond the tunable grace or falls too far behind competing cultural contenders, freeing resources for stronger victory routes.
	- Cultural pursuit gated by strong current or planned war enemies: no point investing heavily in a future cultural victory if the AI may lose the war first. The AI no longer pursues serious culture-victory if its current or planned war enemies reach the shared `bEnemyStrong` power threshold (>=120% enemy power ratio by default) (the local `bDanger` is intentionally absent, because one threatened city should not veto an empire-wide victory strategy), reducing culture-victory pressure on production, specialists, and commerce while preserving ordinary local-border culture. This is not an absolute ban but relative to military high enemy power and cultural advancement: Culture 2 can still remain available against weaker current or planned war opponents, culture can resume after peace if relevant, and a close late cultural win is not abandoned if all target cities already have credible high-culture countdowns. In BBAI tests, a midgame map test showed the rule firing 25 times for Spain at 124% to 288% enemy power, blocking Culture 2+ pursuit despite otherwise meeting the new culture-victory foundation gates; Culture 2 remained available during a weaker war at 67% enemy power and resumed for four consecutive turns after peace.
	- AI prioritizes a more advanced victory plan over an unprepared cultural bid: when an AI has advanced far enough (a tunable AI Victory Stage as of now >= 3 (out of max 4) by default) toward another victory (Space, Conquest, Domination, or Diplomatic), it no longer diverts production, specialists, and commerce into serious cultural-victory spending unless all 3 required Legendary-city candidates are already advanced enough to keep culture credible. Ordinary local-border culture remains unaffected, and an AI already close to winning culturally can still finish the job. In a confirming save file 437 BBAI test, this stopped Rome's serious cultural spending 17 times after it reached Domination 3, when only 1 of its 3 required culture cities was advanced and a cultural win remained 499 to 737 turns away. Rome could still experiment with culture before Domination 3, while Spain remained free to pursue culture because it had no similarly advanced alternative victory.
	- AI abandons clearly impossible cultural deadlines sooner: once all 3 required cities have progressed far enough to produce a complete estimate at the maximum affordable culture slider, the AI now immediately compares its slowest city with the estimated turns remaining instead of waiting for the late game. AI drops from serious Culture 2+ investment to Culture 1 when the estimate exceeds the tunable grace (as of now 125% of the remaining turns): it stops victory-specific cultural spending for now, preserves ordinary local-border culture, and can reconsider later if conditions improve. Before all 3 prospective Legendary cities are developed enough for a reliable estimate, Culture 2 remains available to help the least-developed of them catch up. Unlike the previous change, this does not require another victory plan to be more advanced; an impossible cultural timeline is enough. Another save file 437 test's BBAI logging confirmed Spain entered Culture 2 while its third prospective Legendary city was still too undeveloped for a reliable three-city finish estimate; on one such turn, it raised the culture slider to 30% and gave that lagging city the strongest culture priority. Once all 3 cities could be estimated, even the maximum affordable culture slider projected that Spain needed 398 to 401 turns to win culturally, but only 170 to 171 turns remained, so the AI stopped serious cultural spending.
	- Cultural-victory production focus follows the actual Legendary-city targets: during Culture 2, Base AdvCiv exempted the 4 cities with the highest current culture rates from some routine military production choices, intending to leave more of their production available for cultural development. This could exempt cities that needed no additional cultural help while an actual lagging Legendary-city target continued performing routine production duties. The AI now aligns the culture-production role with victory candidates: it grants this exemption only to the 3 required candidates that still have positive bottleneck-balanced culture investment; other cities remain available for ordinary production. In other save file 437 runs, BBAI logging showed the old rule granted 21 logged production exemptions: 14 involved cities that the balancing logic judged needed no further victory-specific culture, and 6 involved cities outside the selected top 3. After the change, all 4 observed exemptions went to active candidates, and every candidate that qualified received the exemption.
	- AI prioritizes a faster active victory countdown instead of switching to an all-in cultural pursuit: an active victory countdown is more reliable than a projected cultural finish. When the AI has already started such a countdown and it is shorter than the estimated turns needed for its slowest prospective Legendary city, the AI now remains at Culture 1 instead of entering Culture 2/3/4. This prevents victory-specific production, specialist, and commerce spending while preserving ordinary local culture and culture-slider spending needed for happiness. Save file 428 BBAI logging originally showed Mali enter Culture 3 and set its culture slider to 100% despite needing an estimated 106 turns to win culturally while its launched Space victory had only 4 turns remaining. In the confirming run (i.e. after the change), the new rule fired throughout the final `4 -> 1` Space countdown while culture remained 65-64 turns away: Mali stayed at Culture 1, used only 0-30% culture for happiness, ran no culture process, and continued producing military units.

- City Plots (AI)
	- Fix some K-Mod allocation pathologies - Update: Refine the AdvCiv-SAS starvation guard to permit partial recovery: Base AdvCiv blocked a job swap from creating starvation but, once a city was already starving, could allow a swap that worsened its food deficit. An earlier AdvCiv-SAS correction prevented those worsening swaps, but was too strict in the other direction: it rejected every reassignment that left any deficit, even when switching one population member to a higher-food plot substantially reduced starvation. The AI now blocks only food-reducing swaps while a deficit remains; equal-food improvements and partial food recovery can proceed when the full city-aware comparison approves them, while forced and free specialists remain protected. Save file 428 BBAI logging had shown Bursa retain 4-5 Artists while starving by 5-7 food because its available 4-food plot could not eliminate the whole deficit in one reassignment; confirming runs no longer reproduced the severe Bursa and Rome starvation cases.
	- If happiness room is high, push growth harder - Update: now mitigated/halted by high food loss from unhealthiness, where it is inefficient to grow more (e.g., York size 25 with 25 health 36 unhealth (-11 food) and 39 happy 27 unhappy, +3 food per turn still, should not grow more and work specialists or other yields than food rather to increase efficiency).
	- Recheck and reassign citizens to safe, clearly better unworked plots: K-Mod could keep a weaker worked (i.e. assigned to one population in city) plot because its quick initial estimate overlooked another plot that its full city-aware comparison considered better. If ordinary citizen allocation finds no change, the AI now rechecks plots that improve at least one yield without reducing any, trade some production for a large food/commerce gain, or otherwise offer a substantial overall gain without losing food. It switches only when the full city-aware valuation confirms the change is beneficial, never when the swap would cause starvation, and does not immediately reverse a plot assigned earlier in the same update. The newly added `SAS_BBAI_CITIZEN_LOG_LEVEL` BBAI logging test showed this corrected cases such as Karakorum working 2 food / 0 hammers / 1 commerce instead of the strictly better 2 food / 1 hammer / 1 commerce plot, made 624 approved swaps through turn 359 at normal game speed, and reduced remaining plot-to-plot contextual misses from 4,187 to 3,229 (about 23%); specialist-related swaps remain a separate target. Note: for convenience, human cities use the same fallback by default (tunable) through `SAS_CONVENIENCE_HUMAN_CITIZEN_PLOT_FALLBACK_ENABLE`, but only while citizen automation is enabled; manual citizen assignments remain untouched.
	- Recheck modest food trades missed by K-Mod's quick plot estimate: Additionally to the previous change, BBAI logging still found recurring high-value cases such as working 2 food / 1 hammer / 1 commerce instead of 3 food / 0 hammers / 1 commerce, or 3 food / 1 hammer / 1 commerce instead of 4 food / 0 hammers / 1 commerce. The fallback now also considers an unworked plot that provides at least +1 food while losing no more than 1 hammer and 2 commerce, but reassigns the population only when the full city-aware comparison confirms that the trade is beneficial and the existing starvation/stability safeguards pass. Across directionally comparable but diverging Save file 428 BBAI runs through turn 348, this made 780 approved modest-food trades; unresolved records matching the new pattern fell from 1,460 to 0, all remaining plot-to-plot contextual-miss records fell from 3,702 to 2,404, and those valued above 1,000 fell from 762 to 162.
	- Consider additional beneficial food trades missed by K-Mod's quick plot estimate: Additionally to the previous change, BBAI logging still found the preceding narrow limits could still exclude worthwhile changes such as 2 food / 0 hammers / 3 commerce to 3 food / 0 hammers / 0 commerce, even when the city's full valuation strongly preferred the extra food. The fallback now also checks any plot trade that gains at least +1 food without reducing the simple diagnostic score of 3 food + 2 hammers + 1 commerce. This score only identifies candidates: the full city-aware comparison must still approve every reassignment, and the existing starvation and stability safeguards remain in force. In directionally comparable but diverging save file 428 runs through turn 340, the new rule made 292 approved reassignments, eliminated all 372 previously unresolved records matching this pattern, reduced all remaining plot-to-plot misses from 2,434 to 1,936, and reduced those valued above 1,000 from 211 to 88; no new-rule reassignment was reversed within one turn.

- City Production (AI)
	- Optional first economic unit before the capital's first defender: when the first and only capital is not in danger, it can produce one immediately useful Work Boat or required land Worker before filling an empty city garrison. This should be more efficient because it avoids delaying the first improvement and the growth, production, and resources it unlocks, and animals are not a serious city-capture threat. However, because a human can try an unusually early rush against the temporarily empty capital, this tradeoff is XML-tunable and can be disabled to restore Base AdvCiv's defender-first behavior. This is as of now only applicable to the first city, so the unchanged no-defender priority resumes after this first economic unit.
	- Prioritize useful Work Boats for seafood before land Workers: after producing an immediate defender when needed, a safe AI city now builds a Work Boat before its land Worker when it has seafood that can be improved now (unless immediate defense and danger/Turtle safeguards take precedence). This is more efficient because improving seafood increases food without stopping city growth during production, and this extra food helps the following Worker and later production be produced faster. Note: the AI does not waste a Work Boat on unavailable improvements such as Whale before the required technology like as of now `TECH_SEAFARING` is discovered. Note: also remove the Base AdvCiv population-3 cutoff that stopped Work Boat production midway (See KI#174). After these changes, in save file 443 BBAI tests, Seoul successfully selected a Work Boat for its available Molluscs on turn 5 instead of delaying it until about turn 17 (to produce a worker before it), while Mutal correctly built its Worker first because Maya lacked the as of now required `TECH_FISHING` for Molluscs and Fish, then selected a Work Boat on turn 12 when this tech was discovered and thus these bonuses became improvable.
	- Emergency water-food / water-hammer buildings (pre-fallback) - Update: Also force the water-hammer building class (e.g., Port) for high-enough pop (enough headcount so it is worth investing on water plots) coastal cities unless they are very good and don't need it: cities that still have fairly low production or not too high food surplus; Port can take significant time, but these cities are unlikely to contribute much short-term production anyway, and hammer on water tiles makes their later infrastructure and future building time more helpful later on. Immediate danger / defense logic still runs earlier, so Walls/Castle can still go first when needed, otherwise the risk is deemed worth the delay to produce these buildings. Runs in `CvCity::doTurn`.

- Specialists (AI)
	- Add sanity guards Update: Fix AdvCiv-SAS AI specialist assignment growth guard preventing existing specialists from considering switching types (in case they may be better) even though such swaps do not reduce city growth: existing specialists can still switch to a better specialist type because doing so does not reduce worked population or growth. BBAI culture diagnostics exposed this when Barcelona had 8 existing Priests/Merchants but the growth guard forbade considering any switch between specialist types; normal valuation can now compare them and choose another type (e.g. Artist, Engineer, Scientist) when better.
	- Reassign existing AI specialists to a better plot or specialist type: K-Mod's quick initial estimate could leave a city population member assigned as a specialist even when K-Mod's existing full city-aware comparison rated an available plot or another specialist type higher. The AI now applies that full comparison through an additional pass that can reassign the specialist to the better plot or specialist type (an assignable free-specialist slot (counted by `totalFreeSpecialists()`), which does not use a population member, may change to a better allowed type; fixed-type free specialists (`getFreeSpecialistCount(type)`) remain unchanged, and explicitly AI/DLL-forced specialist assignments are not changed by this pass). This pass only reassigns existing specialists; it never removes a population member from a worked city plot to create a new specialist, preserving the AdvCiv-SAS growth-first safeguards against excessive specialist assignment, and every change must still pass the full city-aware valuation and starvation checks. Compared with the preceding BBAI run without this additional reassignment pass, both measured through turn 402, the new run made 1,963 approved reassignments (918 to plots and 1,045 between specialist types) and reduced remaining logged specialist-origin misses from 2,601 to 0. As a directional sanity check within the new run rather than a before/after comparison, this pass made 201 reassignments from Artist to another job and 46 reassignments to Artist.
	- Reduce one-time corporation-founder pressure on recurring specialist selection: Before founding a corporation, K-Mod repeatedly used the full value of its headquarters when deciding how strongly cities should favor specialist jobs that generate the required Great Person type. This helps produce the one founder, but a very valuable headquarters could make the AI assign too many of those specialists while waiting for that single Great Person (e.g., if at war but with many artist assignments). The AI now keeps the full value of eventually using the generated Great Person's Join City action, but counts only 50% (by default, tunable) of the corporation's additional value over Join City toward specialist selection. This reduces excessive pre-founder specialist pressure while retaining an incentive to produce the founder, and makes other specialist types or worked plots more competitive without forbidding the required Great Person type. This applies to every Great Person type that can found a corporation, not only Great Artists. Once generated, the Great Person still evaluates founding the corporation at full value. Behavior after the corporation is founded is unchanged. In the BBAI test, this reduced Corporation 7's contribution to Great Artist selection from 31,987 to 18,089; the corporation remained valuable enough that Rome still generated the required Great Artist on turn 297 and founded it on turn 298.
	- Reduce empire-wide specialist pressure from one city's local Join City culture value, especially for Artists (as this specialist gives most culture): K-Mod uses the best value of having a Great Person Join City anywhere in the empire when weighting every specialist that can generate that Great Person type. BBAI logging showed that one city's high local culture value could therefore make Artists look unusually valuable throughout an empire that was not pursuing cultural victory, including during war: at culture stage 0 with war as its focus, Egypt had 8-9 Artists in Thebes and 7 in Memphis while high Join City culture values elsewhere set its empire-wide Great Artist weight. Outside cultural-victory stages, the AI now counts only 50% of the Join City culture component by default (tunable) when calculating recurring Great Person weights, making other specialist types or worked plots more competitive; all other Join City components keep their full value. The rule checks every Great Person type and reduces any culture its Join City specialist provides, but Great Artists are affected most because their joined specialist provides much more culture. Actual Great Person actions still use full value, and cultural-victory stages 1-4 retain 100% of the culture component. Across directionally comparable but diverging BBAI autoplays, the maximum normalized Great Artist weight fell from 163 to 132 and entries above 130 fell from 111 to 11; the new run still included Join City, Discover, Golden Age, Great Work, and corporation-founding uses of Great Artists.
	- Reduce direct Artist overvaluation in secure, non-cultural AI cities: After reducing empire-wide Great Artist weight pressure, BBAI diagnostics exposed a narrower local issue: wartime Mediolanum still assigned 5-6 Artists despite having full BFC control, no revolt risk, low culture pressure around 104-105, and an unworked 0 food / 7 hammers / 3 commerce plot. In AI job comparisons, clean cities now count only 50% of an Artist specialist's direct culture commerce by default (tunable); the Artist's research, Great Person points, and other value remain unchanged. Full Artist culture value is retained for cultural-victory investment, first-ring expansion, occupation/disorder or revolt risk, emergency culture pressure, an insecure city tile, or any unowned, foreign-owned, or culture-contested BFC plot. In the tested autoplay, the 50% rule appeared in 100 secure-city records containing at most 4 Artists, while all 236 records with at least 5 Artists retained 100% because they still had local culture needs; this verifies the deliberately cautious scope rather than proving a controlled overall reduction.
	- Note as before in previous releases: Exception to the sanity guards rule, and AI enhancement: if a city hasn’t expanded to its BFC yet, auto-assign exactly one Artist to pop borders, then stop once secured. Implemented in `CvCity::AI_doTurn`.

- Civics (AI)
	- Civic-switch damping (anti-oscillation) - Update: Additionally, BBAI diagnostics showed the expensive remaining problem was paid-anarchy civic churn, especially direct reversals soon after the normal civic timer expired. Civic switches that add anarchy now need a larger value gain, and paid-anarchy revolutions add a longer cooldown before civics are reconsidered; free/no-extra-anarchy bundled changes remain more responsive. In the tested run, paid-anarchy revolutions dropped from 52 to 23, total anarchy turns from 174 to 81, and both-sides-paid direct reversals from 49 to 17 (See KI#159). This should be stronger, considering excessive anarchy downtime is inefficient, especially with our as of now AdvCiv-SAS anarchy duration of 2 turns.

- Barbarians (AI)
	- New World barbarian aggression toggle: preserve both the old (as of now removed in Base AdvCiv) and the new Base AdvCiv behaviors via the `SAS_AI_BARBARIAN_NEW_WORLD_EARLIER_ATTACK_ENABLE` SAS define: The default `1` as of now uses the newer city/population presence rule that can end the local peace period earlier; the old as of now disabled `0` restores the older peaceable population rule.
	- Barbarian Workers no longer retreat to the city they are already inside: When a Barbarian Worker had no useful job and was already safe in a Barbarian city, Base AdvCiv still ordered it to "retreat" to that same city every turn. This trapped it in a pointless retreat/wait loop. It now simply waits for one turn (or may be scrapped when allowed), then checks again for useful work. See KI#175.

- Units (Common) (AI)
	- Old default scrapping mode is removed or simplified: further restrict AI scrapping rules, now and as of now only to sufficiently obsolete units.
	- Remove old hardcoded AI scrap era/XMLcost rules (new recent rules are more dynamic, easier, less fragile to change/maintenance, and likely stronger for AI too)
	- Scrapping update: added an optional (as of now disabled) absolute XML kill switch to disable all AI scrapping for testing or preference. This is separate from the normal scrapping optimization toggle, so disabling optimization does not accidentally restore broad scrapping.
	- Add one for limiting it to obsolete units after an ObsoleteTech-era delay (i.e. block all AI scrapping, except for units that have been obsolete for one or a few eras). Example: as of now Axemen obsolete at Civil Service can still be useful through Medieval, but can be scrapped from Renaissance; Knights obsolete in Renaissance can remain useful through Renaissance, but can be scrapped from Industrial. Keep cargo-capable units protected and improve scrap diagnostics with separate SCRAP_DECISION and SCRAP_EVENT log lines. The rationale is that only very outdated units are no longer useful enough to justify their clutter or upkeep; until then, keeping units is usually better than short-term financial relief from scrapping, because they can defend cities, deter attacks, pressure rivals, or be spent in war. War can also reduce excess units naturally, and successful captures can increase unit support, economy, and long-term growth more than deleting troops would. So this strict-but-not-absolute new scrapping gate is believed to be stronger for AI.
	- Scrapping update: Work Boat overproduction bug follow-up: after fixing the old Work Boat produce/scrap loop, BBAI logs still showed some minor remaining Work Boat overproduction, confirmed by ingame advisors (e.g., Augustus having built 34 Work Boats by T373, more than any other unit type). Two mismatches were fixed. First, cities could decide whether to build a Work Boat using a different water area than the one used to count seafood targets, so need and availability did not always match. Second, Work Boats could skip owned off-BFC seafood even though city production had counted it as a valid target. These mismatches are now fixed. Following this, Work Boat production is reduced (e.g., to ~27-29 Work Boats for Augustus, remaining seeming to be due to heavy pillaging). As an extra enhancement, stale Work Boats now also skip seafood already connected by another Work Boat. See KI#157.

- Civilian Units (AI): Settlers (AI)
	- Unowned bonus extra valuation in city-site valuation: for example, if we already own Wheat, a site with 1 Maize is much better for the whole empire than a city site with 2 more Wheat, which would have more yields locally for this city (e.g., ~+3-4 extra food per plot), but much less long-term empire growth (e.g., +1 Health +1 Health with Granary for all cities) (which is perhaps better even for this local city). Or, stone, happiness, or other bonuses could provide other advantages, for example trading, military, accelerating buildables, etc. Trade imports do not count as owned because they are not permanent. Applied once per bonus type and only after the first city. See KI#147.
	- Settler site eval - devalue BFCs with many very bad tiles - Update: Candidate sites are significantly devalued (not auto-rejected) when (Requires AdvCiv-SAS 5977+) enough non-home BFC plots are impassable or remain very low-yield after their best XML-valid improvement: the threshold as of now is dynamic `4 * food + 2 * production + commerce` (e.g., 1 food is not very bad, 1 hammer and 1 commerce is very bad, peak is very bad) (visible bonuses on usable plots are not counted because bonuses are likely to be always valuable). Also no longer hardcoded so it is more flexible/future-proof.
	- Low-food environments - Update: evaluate food scarcity from each non-home BFC plot's natural food and food added by bonus-specific improvements (e.g., tundra is low food, peak is very low food, but if there are bonuses that give food in the site it reduces the net food-scarcity score (e.g., high tundra high fish/deer may be better than grass/plain hybrid no food from bonuses so do not discard it)). Also no longer hardcoded score value: now derives from actual yields, so resistant to XML modifications.
	- First-city scoring: value usable food bonuses, fresh-water city tiles, and river support more for the capital than for ordinary later city sites, because early capital growth snowballs into faster tile access, worker/settler production, whipping, and specialists. See KI#144.
	- Removed map-generation spacing bias from live first-settler choices: keeping players' assigned starts far enough apart makes sense while generating the map, but after play begins their settlers should be free to found wherever they evaluate as best, whether closer to or farther from rivals. BBAI logging in save file 442 showed that Berlin's greener, resource-rich `(33,13)` site was valued higher than the `(35,11)` (4569 to 3934), but the old map-generation spacing penalties of -1482 versus -480 reversed them to 3087 versus 3454. Removing this major cause of missed strong city sites helped but did not complete the Berlin fix alone; the smaller bonus-scoring and scouting changes as of now below then let the AI settler fully reveal and normally rank the stronger site, ultimately choosing `(33,13)` successfully without a Berlin-specific rule. Old Base AdvCiv runtime behavior can optionally be restored (not recommended). See KI#173.
	- Simplified bonus-improvement yield valuation: disabled AdvCiv's obscure nonlinear `evaluateSpecialYields` aggregation after the new SAS valuation made it redundant and retaining both paths heavily overscored bonus-rich sites. The inherited path combined many hardcoded fixed-point weights, context branches, and another food modifier; it was difficult to understand and could only be tuned by editing and recompiling the DLL. In the save file 360 Karakorum BBAI test, tundra-heavy `(49,43)` with Pig, 3 Elephants, and 2 Fur scored 6934 overall (7037 after more tiles were revealed), including 5015 resource points and 4239 from the old special-yield path. It therefore incorrectly beat the nearby river/Grass area: for example, Pig + Maize `(52,40)` scored only 4836. After simplification, `(49,43)` fell to 3835 overall, including 2126 resource points and 1350 from actual bonus-improvement yields; it then lost to nearby Pig + Maize river Grassland `(50,40)` at 4502, which the AI settler chose. The new capital has stronger food, river support, and greener long-term workable land instead of being pulled toward low-food Tundra by duplicate Elephants and Fur. Actual Food, Production, and Commerce added by bonus improvements are now valued and tunable only once through the existing simple SAS defines (e.g., explicitly for Tundra or Flood Plains). Note: Bonus plots also receive the same tunable terrain valuation as non-bonus plots, so e.g., Iron on Grass is as of now valued higher than Iron on Snow.
	- Made Base AdvCiv's inherited natural-yield weights tunable: Self-Sustaining, One-Food-Short, and Default Food/Production/Commerce values are now exposed through SAS XML defines. These actual-yield weights currently overlap with explicit SAS terrain/feature preferences such as Grass, Tundra, and Flood Plains; this is acceptable for now because those SAS values act as strategic adjustments on top of real yields, but the overlap can be revisited later.
	- Removed Base AdvCiv's separate first-era Commerce boost from city-site scoring: city sites persist into later eras, Commerce is already valued normally, and early Food and Production are at least as important because they drive growth, workers, settlers, defense, and military; the same fundamentals apply to Barbarian cities. Applying an extra Commerce value only while evaluating the first two cities in the first era added complexity without a coherent long-term settlement priority.
	- Similarly, removed Base AdvCiv's small Barbarian production bias from city-site scoring: Barbarian cities still need Food for growth and Commerce for research/economy, while normal scoring already values Production and Barbarian military priorities are handled elsewhere. Slightly reducing Food and Commerce to favor Production did not justify a separate tile-yield valuation layer, especially for such minor values.
	- First-city roaming - Update: Since the first city is especially important, unlike later cities that can be more strategic or semi-decent resource grabs, starting settlers can now roam briefly for a better first-city site when the current site is bad enough. Settlers can spend the bounded early window to move to visible good-enough sites, do safe adjacent scouting for better information, or wait if no safe scouting step exists. Starting-site checks use a shared potential-food environment score (using the same logic described under Low-food environments). Food-surplus plots offset poor plots, productive Grass Hills remain neutral, and no-yield peaks/ice receive an extra citizen-unworkable penalty because they are dead BFC slots. This keeps useful resources/coasts from looking poor while still moving away from bad tundra/plains/peak-heavy starts.
	- First-city recheck and efficient return after scouting: After leaving a very poor spawn location, the AI settler explores nearby unrevealed plots (fog) within a limited scouting window (in turns) instead of founding the first merely good-enough site, trying to find better first-city sites, like a human player would. Only fully revealed BFCs can interrupt scouting; before each further step, the AI settler protects the strongest newly discovered reachable site by full city-site value, with the tunable return-path cost breaking exact ties. If the raw-value winner is instead the poor scout origin it deliberately left, sites are compared after subtracting their return-path cost so a nearby partly revealed candidate can finish being explored instead of needlessly undoing the scouting trip. The turns window includes the trip back, so the AI settler commits before further scouting would make its strongest return too late, then queues movement and founding together instead of restarting scouting on arrival or founding wherever scouting stopped. Once this window has ended, return-path cost can still favor a nearly equal nearby site over lengthy backtracking. Empirically and as BBAI logging also showed, this helped Berlin, Karakorum, Cuzco, and the Aztecs choose strong revealed sites and found promptly in save files 442, 360, and 431. As of now this applies only to the first city, which critically benefits from extra information and founds much stronger first-city sites, while later AI settlers usually know more of the local map and so do not need to use this (so as of now disabled for them). See KI#144.
	- Optional naval-heavy-map coastal settling bias: Naval-heavy maps can now add a tunable first-city or later-city preference for coastal settling in naval-heavy maps. - Civilian Units (AI): Workers (AI)
	- Outside-BFC fort-airbase path disabled by default: AdvCiv-SAS worker logic already protects BFC plots from fort choices which are deemed inefficient so never chosen; this also optionally disables the old late-game path where workers could build forts outside BFC for airbase/canal use: we prefer not risking future irrigation-chain paths or spending worker turns on rarely useful defensive modifiers, scattered airbases, or fort canal/isthmus effects.
	- Reliable Worker availability without early overproduction: AI cities rebuild enough Workers for each land area before other production priorities can leave them short indefinitely, without producing more than the AI calculates it needs. Maintaining enough Workers throughout the game lets the AI improve food/production/commerce, connect resources and routes, repair pillaging, and develop new or underdeveloped cities sooner, especially after Worker losses or earlier underproduction. This normally aims for 1 Worker per city plus a capital-area reserve. As a refinement, a one-city empire needs at most 1 land Worker until its capital has ever reached a tunable population (5 by default), avoiding early double-Worker production and the resulting halted growth; normal demand resumes after the capital matures or another city is founded. Ordinary Worker choices, cases where a blocked Settler choice falls back to a Worker, general unit selection, and Worker scrapping now use the same centralized target. Four BBAI test runs then completed exactly 1 early Worker per empire, with the only later automatic second Worker beginning when its capital reached population 5.
	- Heavily refactored, fixed, enhanced, and made tunable SAS Worker AI logic now in SAS defines. See notably: https://github.com/wonderingabout/AdvCiv-SAS/pull/26/changes. Among the changes
		- pass 2: Fix low value improvements being skipped instead of treated as low-priority and other changes: Hill Tundra, Flat Snow and Flat Desert are no longer NONE: They now have tiny fallback values, reduced growth scaling to 400 * growth level (so food is a bit more important than keeping growth improvements especially higher level ones and later in the game where we have more specialist or such yields)
		- pass 3: Evaluate all valid no-bonus improvement candidates before choosing one, then pick the highest adjusted value through one generic picker. This replaces hardcoded if/else priority order with XML branch base values plus Farm/Cottage/Workshop/Mine/Windmill value adjustments, so low-value candidates remain low priority instead of being skipped. Allows to make code simpler too.
		- pass 5: fix non-bonus Fallout handling by moving it out of the Jungle branch into its own feature-removal path, so scrub priority is explicit and ordinary non-bonus builds are skipped when Fallout cannot be scrubbed yet.
		- pass 6: Generalize the old Camp/Deer/Elephants/Fur feature-preserving path to use the XML-inferred bonus-specific build when it preserves Forest/Jungle, while keeping Mine/Plantation-style builds on the explicit feature-removal path.
		- pass 7: Remove the special Workshop allow gate and let no-bonus branch XML values compete normally. Food-cost improvements now receive a generic recovered-food bonus only after current plot/player rules make them food-neutral or better, so early food-cost Workshops stay conservative while later food-neutral Workshops can still become attractive.
	- Increase the XML-tunable enough-food Grass and Plains Hill Mine values: SAS_WORKER_AI_BASE_BRANCH_TERRAIN_GRASS_HILL (BUILD_MINE:1590->1700), SAS_WORKER_AI_BASE_BRANCH_TERRAIN_PLAINS_HILL (BUILD_MINE:800->1600)
	- Yield-first build execution: workers build the exact improvement selected through the full city/plot evaluation instead of letting the older `AI_betterPlotBuild` postprocessing replace a scored Chop, Mine, Farm, etc. with an unrelated Road. Roads/routes remain strongly de-prioritized early unless they are truly the best move, while important connectable bonuses are still connected. See KI#31.
	- Overhaul: `AI_bestCityBuild` (full routine) - Update: concrete dry-farm irrigation-chain fixes can replace the least harmful blocker first; and the logic is more mindful of preserving useful irrigation paths instead of blindly maximizing immediate cottage/workshop yield (Reserve likely irrigation/farm connector plots from non-farm builds to reduce AI worker farm/non-farm oscillation).
	- Worker lower-food replacement guard: from a tunable era onward (default as of now: Medieval), AI workers no longer replace existing no-bonus improvements with lower-food alternatives. This targets repeated food-threshold oscillation such as Farm → Cottage → Farm → Cottage or Mine → Windmill → Mine after the city briefly looks food-safe. It still allows equal-food retools, food-restoration changes such as Cottage → Farm, and cleanup of City Ruins or obsolete/non-active improvements. In the tested BBAI log, the worst Mali farm/cottage loops reached Farm again and then skipped later Farm → Cottage attempts from about T128/T130 onward. This change is because before this era, cities are often small or health/happiness-capped (extra/excess food may not necessarily help or could be inefficient early) and early cottages still have time to mature, but later, cities are usually larger, health sources are generally multiple, and specialist yields are stronger than plot yields (so any food can be used and is generally strong/stronger), and cottages/growth improvements have less time to grow and pay back, so preserving food support is usually stronger.
	- Feature-removal health handling in `CvUnitAI::AI_bestCityBuild` - Update: For medium/large cities, non-bonus forested BFC plots have a softer health penalty. This is because later in the game we usually have many health sources (trade, owned bonuses, buildings, techs, etc.), higher population can use or needs more improved BFC plots, and improved yields are stronger, so leaving useful forested tiles unimproved forever can be worse than accepting the forest-health loss.
	- Compare the best useful work across all cities: workers now compare each city's strongest reachable job by its complete Food/Production/Commerce/resource/terrain evaluation, with only a modest travel cost, instead of favoring cities mainly by population or staying near weaker local work. Cities that lack enough completed or already-assigned improvements for their population receive proportionally more help; counting assigned workers discourages crowding one city, while reserving one worker per plot prevents several workers from targeting the same tile. In BBAI testing in save file 442, this redirected workers from low-value unused Tundra Workshops toward valuable Grass/Plains Hill Mines in underdeveloped cities.
	- Worker mobility / overimprovement refinement - Update: The small-city rotation shortcut does not make a worker leave when it is already standing on this same city's useful BFC plot and that plot is the city's best current job.
	- Finish valuable followup builds after feature removal: a worker can complete standalone Forest/Jungle/Fallout removal (including receiving Forest chop production sooner, separated from e.g. a 2 in 1 mine that would deliver hammer on feature remove later plus would be less flexible), then continue directly into the worthwhile Mine, Farm, Cottage, resource improvement, etc. selected for that plot. This prevents workers from chopping or clearing a strong tile, walking away, and paying another trip later (which is especially inefficient if this costs movement/delay again); weak ordinary follow-ups are still reconsidered instead of being forced. Queued follow-ups now wait for the worker's next turn when removal consumes all movement rather than being silently discarded. See also KI#176.
	- Worker irrigation-chain hunting: updated SAS AI worker logic so it now starts from actual dry-farm / low-food BFC targets and hunts short owned irrigation-chain steps, including first links outside the city BFC. This fixed Chaco Canyon and Harappan-style cases where a nearby (2-3 plots away from BFC) water source existed but BFC-only worker logic missed the first link. Workers now retain only the strongest available connector toward each target instead of treating several alternative routes to the same Farm as separate urgent jobs, leaving other workers free to pursue valuable improvements elsewhere.

- Military (AI)
	- City evacuation — doomed city auto-retreat (land units only) - Update: Fixed an inherited Base AdvCiv percentile inversion that instead gave this important-city protection to low-value cities; see KI#172. Also clarified and renamed doomed-city all-land-unit SAS define (that it requires to exceed the existing `AI_EVACUATION_THRESH`-based (default as of now: 138%) attacker/defender strength threshold)
	- Unit upgrades - prefer the important domain for the map: AI unit-upgrade value now optionally uses the existing SAS map-script heaviness logic to relatively value unit upgrades (not a hard ban unless relative value is set high): the real gold price is unchanged; this makes the AI think actual upgrade cost is higher for these unit upgrades. As a result, on land-heavy maps, naval upgrades are treated as lower priority, so the AI is more likely to spend gold upgrading land units first instead of modernizing old ships that have little strategic value on that map. On naval-heavy maps, the same idea can apply in reverse for land upgrades.
	- Remote captured-city attack stacks: large AI attack stacks in faraway captured cities no longer let upgrade waiting override a ready offensive target. If the stack is large enough, has a valid target, and the target is not too strong, it keeps using the existing attack movement logic instead of parking most of the empire's army far from its core because several units could upgrade, which was very problematic. In the BBAI-log sample, the Celtic Boston trapped-stack pattern was visible from T156 to T256; the stricter remote-trap diagnostic confirmed it from T172 to T214, with up to 41 units / 62% of Celtic military parked in a faraway captured city while still having ready targets. See KI#155.
	- Ready attack stacks stop parking for unavailable upgrades: if a city-attack stack is ready, the target is not too strong, and 0 units can upgrade now, the AI now keeps moving toward the target instead of waiting for possible future upgrades. This fixes real offensive freezes: in one BBAI-log sample, USA Washington waited from T147-T181 with up to 59 units / 69% of total military while targets were only 3-5 turns away; Mali Gao waited from T233-T255 with up to 42 units / 41% of total military. The AI still waits for real immediate upgrades: a USA Seattle stack waited while 6 units could upgrade now, then attacked/moved once that number became 0. This is thought to be much stronger, because it uses the current military advantage before enemies catch up; old units can be spent in war instead of sitting around draining upkeep, needing future upgrade gold (solved if they die as well), and risking obsolescence; successful captures can then increase economy, unit support, and long-term growth; and the needless wait was very problematic and inefficient too. See KI#156.
	- AI unit upgrades - stop routine upgrades from draining the treasury: Fixes an AI spending problem where normal unit upgrades could overshoot the intended upgrade budget. Previously, the AI only checked whether it was still below budget before starting the next upgrade, but did not check whether that next upgrade itself fit the remaining budget. For example, in BBAI diagnostics, an AI with 280 gold reserved for upgrades could spend 237 gold, still count as under budget, then start another 237 gold upgrade and end up spending 474 gold total, sometimes leaving only a few gold in the treasury. Routine upgrades now check the actual remaining upgrade budget before upgrading. Emergency-style upgrades, such as important city defenders or special impassable-unit upgrades, remain more flexible, but normal upgrades are much less likely to waste all available gold. AIs in financial trouble also avoid routine upgrades unless they are focused on war. See KI#160.
	- AI attack stacks now opportunistically capture nearby barbarian cities: before this fix, ready city-attack-capable (i.e. not just `UNITAI_ATTACK_CITY`) stacks could sit idle because the AI had no normal war target yet, even with a nearby pathable barbarian city. Now, if the AI has no normal target and is not already busy with an active war, the stack can attack that barbarian city instead. This does not override normal wars or normal war targets; it fills the no-target idle gap. This change is usually a strong move: barbarian cities are often weak, cost little diplomatically, and capturing them can give land, population, resources, unit support, and future growth without spending a settler or interrupting city production nor growth. In the tested save before the fix, nearby barbarian cities survived at game end at ~T400 despite reachable AI armies to Napoleon and Shaka, and barbarian being very weak in comparison, yet bonuses-rich, and Shaka was among weakest players; after the fix, Zulu captured Hurrian at T127, Alemanni at T132, Visigoth at T148, and Gaul at T153, turning those nearby barb cities into an early expansion boost and making Shaka the strongest player for almost all middle game and end game, almost allowing him to win. This change should make AI significantly stronger. See KI#158.

	- Expanded handicap difficulty ladder: added Rookie below Settler and Deity+ above Deity. Rookie gives new/casual players a softer entry point (AdvCiv-SAS Settler is harder and more gradual to other higher handicaps and less of a tutorial sandbox than base AdvCiv), while Deity+ gives expert players an extra challenge above Deity (AdvCiv-SAS Deity has less cheats and more gradual to previous handicaps).
	- Cleaner handicap difficulty rating scale: reworked `iDifficulty` from the old Settler-to-Deity `10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90` curve to a new Rookie-to-Deity+ `18, 26, 34, 42, 50, 58, 66, 74, 82, 90, 98` curve. This keeps Noble at `50` as the even/baseline difficulty, and places the new lower/higher difficulties around that scale without using `0` or values above `100` (`iDifficulty` is not the main source of direct AI bonuses or player penalties; those still come from fields like `iAIResearchPercent`, `iAITrainPercent`, maintenance, happiness, barbarian modifiers, and so on. Instead, `iDifficulty` is a general difficulty rating used by scoring, comparisons, and code that needs to average or estimate handicap strength).
	- More robust handicap averaging: updated DLL code that converts an averaged difficulty score back into a handicap. Previously, some code effectively averaged raw handicap enum/order IDs, which only works reliably when difficulty rows are fixed and evenly spaced. It now averages XML `iDifficulty` values and maps back to the nearest handicap, so added or future non-linear handicap levels behave according to the XML difficulty rating rather than accidental enum position.
	- Explicit and tighter barbarian handicap fields: added explicit values for previously missing barbarian-related handicap fields (`iSeaBarbarianBonus`, `iBarbarianCityAttackBonus`, `iSeaBarbarianExtraMoves`) and tightened their low-difficulty curves. 	- Lower and more gradual barbarian/animal handicap protection: retuned several barbarian and animal handicap modifiers so only the lowest difficulties protect players as of now. For example, `iAnimalBonus`, `iAIAnimalBonus`, `iBarbarianBonus`, and `iAIBarbarianBonus` now follow a clean `-30`/`-20`/`-10`/`0` curve for Rookie/Settler/Chieftain/Warlord and then stay neutral at higher handicaps. In base AdvCiv, several of these protections lasted much farther up the ladder: `iAnimalBonus` still gave `-25` at Noble and remained non-neutral through Immortal, while `iAIAnimalBonus` and `iAIBarbarianBonus` stayed at `-25` all the way from Settler through Deity. This keeps the lowest difficulties forgiving, gradually less so, while removing broad barbarian/animal freebies from most difficulties.
	- More difficulty-sensitive barbarian city creation: reworked `iBarbarianCityCreationProb` from base AdvCiv’s very flat curve into a wider Rookie-to-Deity+ scaling curve. Base AdvCiv only rises from `4` on Settler to `8` on Deity, while AdvCiv-SAS now scales from `0` on Rookie to `40` on Deity+.
